1. PROJECT OVERVIEW
================================================================================

The `prompts/` directory contains four text files that serve as dynamic
templates for interacting with Large Language Models (LLMs), specifically
Google Gemini Pro, within the EWS Retention Platform. These prompts are
fundamental for generating context-aware and personalized advice, summaries,
or messages tailored for different stakeholders (faculty, marketing, sales teams,
and students themselves). They ensure that the LLM's responses are relevant,
structured, and align with the specific needs of each audience.

All four prompt templates are fully implemented and actively used by the
backend's AI advisor service. Each prompt is loaded dynamically based on
the `prompt_name` parameter passed to the `get_ai_advice()` function.


================================================================================
2. PROMPT FILES
================================================================================

Each prompt file defines a specific interaction with the LLM, meticulously
tailored to a particular audience and purpose. They share a common set of
six dynamic placeholders that are populated with real-time student data:

  - `{student_name}`: Full name of the student.
  - `{risk_score}`: The student's calculated risk score (0-100).
  - `{retention_likelihood}`: Human-readable retention likelihood
    (e.g., "Very High", "High", "Medium", "Low", "Very Low").
  - `{grades}`: Overall grade average.
  - `{attendance}`: Overall attendance rate.
  - `{engagement_score}`: Overall engagement score.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.1 faculty_advice.txt
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

- **Location**: `prompts/faculty_advice.txt`

- **Purpose**: To generate personalized advice and actionable conversation
  starters for faculty members who are engaging with at-risk students.

- **Role**: "Expert academic advisor with years of experience in student
  retention and success strategies."

- **Context**: The LLM is told it is advising a faculty member about a
  specific student who may be at risk of dropping out, and should provide
  constructive, empathetic, and proactive support recommendations.

- **Required Output Format** (4 sections):
    1. **Summary of Situation**: A brief, gentle summary of the student's
       current standing based on the provided data points.
    2. **Key Areas of Concern**: Identification of the top 1-2 areas
       contributing most to the student's risk score.
    3. **Recommended Actions & Conversation Starters**: 3-4 specific,
       actionable steps the faculty member can take, each accompanied by
       a non-judgmental, supportive conversation starter phrase.
    4. **University Resources to Suggest**: At least 2 relevant university
       resources the faculty member can recommend to the student (e.g.,
       Tutoring Center, Writing Center, Counseling Services, Financial Aid,
       Career Services).

- **Tone Directive**: Constructive, empathetic, proactive support. Avoids
  blaming language and focuses on how to help the student succeed.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.2 marketing_advice.txt
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

- **Location**: `prompts/marketing_advice.txt`

- **Purpose**: To generate personalized re-engagement communication strategies
  and messaging for the marketing team to reach at-risk students. Used for
  retention campaigns, targeted outreach, and student re-engagement efforts.

- **Role**: "Strategic marketing and communications expert specializing in
  student success and retention in higher education."

- **Context**: The LLM is told it is crafting a personalized communication
  strategy for a specific at-risk student, with the goal of re-engaging them,
  informing them of available support services, and encouraging persistence
  toward degree completion.

- **Required Output Format** (structured communication plan):
    1. **Suggested Communication Channel**: The most appropriate channel
       for reaching this student (Email, SMS, Personalized Portal Message),
       based on the student's engagement patterns.
    2. **Headline / Subject Line**: A catchy, empathetic subject line that
       encourages the student to open and read the message.
    3. **Personalized Message Body** containing:
       - A supportive opening acknowledgment ("We're here for you" tone)
       - Indirect acknowledgment of the student's current challenges
         (without referencing risk scores or internal metrics directly)
       - 1-2 relevant university support services based on the student's
         specific risk factors
       - A clear, low-barrier call to action (e.g., "Click here to book
         a 15-minute chat with your advisor")
       - Positive, encouraging closing tone

- **Tone Directive**: Warm, supportive, non-intrusive. The student should
  feel cared for, not monitored.

- **Placeholders**: Uses the standard 6 placeholders (`{student_name}`,
  `{risk_score}`, `{retention_likelihood}`, `{grades}`, `{attendance}`,
  `{engagement_score}`).

2.3 sales_advice.txt
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

- **Location**: `prompts/sales_advice.txt`

- **Purpose**: To provide guidance, talking points, and objection-handling
  strategies for the sales/admissions team when conducting urgent intervention
  conversations with at-risk students or their guardians. Especially critical
  for high-risk students where continued enrollment is at stake.

- **Role**: "Student success and retention specialist adept at communicating
  value and encouraging commitment to educational goals."

- **Context**: The LLM is told it is preparing a sales/retention team member
  for a conversation with a specific at-risk student (or their guardian),
  with the goal of persuading them to persist and highlighting the value
  of degree completion.

- **Required Output Format** (4 sections):
    1. **Core Message / Objective**: A clear, concise primary goal statement
       for the conversation (e.g., "Re-engage the student by addressing
       their specific concerns and highlighting the long-term value of
       completing their degree").
    2. **Key Talking Points**: 3-5 structured talking points including:
       - Empathy and acknowledgment of the student's situation
       - Value proposition of degree completion
       - Available support systems and resources
       - Addressing specific concerns indicated by the data
       - Instilling hope and a forward-looking perspective
    3. **Call to Action**: A clear, manageable next step for the student
       (e.g., "Schedule a meeting with your academic advisor this week").
    4. **Handling Objections**: Pre-prepared responses to common student
       objections such as:
       - "I don't have enough time"
       - "It's too late to catch up"
       - "I can't afford to continue"
       - "The coursework is too difficult"

- **Tone Directive**: Persuasive but empathetic. Focuses on the student's
  long-term benefit, never on institutional metrics.

- **Placeholders**: Uses the standard 6 placeholders.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.4 student_specific_advice.txt
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

- **Location**: `prompts/student_specific_advice.txt`

- **Purpose**: To generate direct, empathetic, and actionable advice that
  is delivered directly to the student. Used for personalized emails, chatbot
  interactions, or within the student-facing portal. The goal is to empower
  students to take ownership of their academic journey and improve outcomes.

- **Role**: "Empathetic and knowledgeable student success coach specializing
  in personalized academic strategies."

- **Context**: The LLM is told it is writing a message directly to a student,
  providing personalized guidance based on their current academic standing.
  The message should feel like it comes from a caring mentor, not a system.

- **Required Output Format** (cohesive paragraph format, NO section labels):
  The response is formatted as a single, flowing message to the student
  containing these elements woven together naturally:
    1. **Empathy Opening**: Acknowledges the student's challenges without
       condescension. Uses first-person address ("I can see you've been
       working hard...").
    2. **Key Areas Summary**: Gently identifies 1-2 areas where the student
       could improve, framed constructively (e.g., "One area where a small
       change could make a big difference is...").
    3. **Actionable Steps**: 2-3 specific, practical steps the student can
       take immediately, within their direct control (e.g., "Try spending
       just 30 minutes reviewing this week's material before the next quiz").
    4. **Resource Suggestions**: 1-2 specific university support services
       relevant to the student's situation.
    5. **Encouraging Close**: Reaffirms belief in the student's potential
       and the university's commitment to their success.

- **Tone Directive**: Non-judgmental, future-oriented, warm, and supportive.
  Avoids jargon, risk scores, or internal metric language. The student
  should feel motivated, not alarmed.

- **Placeholders**: Uses the standard 6 placeholders.

3. PROMPT TEMPLATE STRUCTURE
================================================================================

All four prompt templates follow a consistent internal structure:

```
Role:
You are a [specific role description]...

Context:
[Scenario description and objective]...

Student Information:
- Name: {student_name}
- Risk Score: {risk_score}/100
- Retention Likelihood: {retention_likelihood}
- Grades: {grades}
- Attendance: {attendance}
- Engagement Score: {engagement_score}

Task:
[Specific instructions for what to generate]...

Output Format:
[Explicit formatting guidelines]...
```

This consistent structure ensures:
- Predictable LLM behavior across all prompt types
- Easy maintenance and updates to prompt templates
- Consistent placeholder names for automated population
- Clear separation of role, context, data, and instructions

4. IMPLEMENTATION & INTEGRATION
================================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.1 AI Advisor Service (backend/app/services/ai/advisor.py)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The `AI Advisor Service` is the primary consumer of these prompt templates.
Its `get_ai_advice()` function orchestrates the following workflow:

1.  **Dependency Check**: Verifies that the `google-generativeai` library is
    installed and that the `GEMINI_API_KEY` is configured in the environment.

2.  **Prompt Loading**: Dynamically loads the appropriate prompt template file
    based on the `prompt_name` parameter:
    ```python
    prompt_path = f"prompts/{prompt_name}_advice.txt"
    ```
    Valid `prompt_name` values: "faculty", "marketing", "sales", "student_specific"

3.  **Data Retrieval**: Fetches the student's latest prediction record and
    other relevant data from the database:
    - Student name (first_name + last_name)
    - Risk score (from latest Prediction record)
    - Retention likelihood (derived from risk_score via RiskScorer)
    - Grades (from graded components average)
    - Attendance (from exam attendance rate)
    - Engagement score (from feature engineering calculations)

4.  **Prompt Formatting**: Populates the placeholders within the loaded prompt
    template using Python's `str.format()`:
    ```python
    formatted_prompt = template.format(
        student_name=student_name,
        risk_score=risk_score,
        retention_likelihood=retention_likelihood,
        grades=grades,
        attendance=attendance,
        engagement_score=engagement_score
    )
    ```

5.  **LLM Invocation**: Calls the Google Gemini Pro model:
    ```python
    model = genai.GenerativeModel('gemini-pro')
    response = model.generate_content(formatted_prompt)
    ```
    Configuration: temperature=0.7, max_tokens=1024 (from .env settings).

6.  **Response Handling**: Returns the generated text from the LLM, or a
    descriptive error message if any issues occur (e.g., missing API key,
    file not found, API call failure, empty response).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.2 LLM Routes (backend/routes/llm.py)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The LLM router exposes several endpoints that internally leverage the prompt
templates:

- **POST /llm/student-advice**: Accepts a `StudentAdviceRequest` with
  `student_id` and optional `question`. Calls the advisor service with
  the `student_specific` prompt.

- **POST /llm/faculty-summary**: Generates faculty-oriented advice using
  the `faculty` prompt template.

- **POST /llm/intervention-package**: Generates multi-channel intervention
  messaging that may use the `marketing` and `sales` prompt templates.

- **POST /llm/explain-risk-change**: Uses a dynamically constructed prompt
  (not template-based) to explain risk score changes.

- **POST /llm/what-if-analysis**: Uses the risk formula to simulate
  scenario changes (not template-based).

- **POST /llm/report-narrative**: Generates executive report narratives
  using a dynamically constructed prompt.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.3 Advisor Routes (backend/routes/advisor.py)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The advisor router provides higher-level AI-powered endpoints:

- **POST /advisor/study-plan/{student_id}**: Generates a personalized
  4-week study plan with weekly goals and estimated hours. Uses the
  `student_specific` prompt as a foundation, then applies additional
  study plan formatting instructions.

- **POST /advisor/faculty-summary/{student_id}**: Generates a faculty-
  appropriate summary with optional talking points. Uses the `faculty`
  prompt template with optional `include_talking_points=true` parameter.

- **GET /advisor/context/{student_id}**: Returns raw advisor context data
  (student metrics, risk factors, engagement data) without LLM processing.

5. DYNAMIC PLACEHOLDER SYSTEM
================================================================================

The placeholder population flow:

```
Database (Student, Enrollment, Prediction tables)
    |
    v
StudentService.calculate_student_metrics()
    |
    v
Six placeholder values extracted:
    {student_name}          <- Student.first_name + " " + Student.last_name
    {risk_score}            <- RiskScorer.calculate_risk_score(features)
    {retention_likelihood}  <- RiskScorer.get_retention_likelihood(risk_score)
    {grades}                <- FeatureEngineer.calculate_graded_quiz_score()
    {attendance}            <- FeatureEngineer.calculate_exam_attendance_rate()
    {engagement_score}      <- Weighted average of all engagement features
    |
    v
Template loaded from prompts/{prompt_name}_advice.txt
    |
    v
str.format() populates placeholders
    |
    v
Formatted prompt sent to Google Gemini Pro
    |
    v
LLM response returned to API caller
```
